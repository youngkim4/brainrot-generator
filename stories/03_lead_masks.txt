Two men were found dead on a hillside in Brazil, wearing suits and homemade lead masks over their eyes. Next to them was a notebook that read, "Ingest capsules. Wait for the signal." No one knows what signal they were waiting for.

On August 20th, 1966, Manoel Pereira da Cruz and Miguel Jose Viana traveled a hundred and sixty miles to Niteroi, Brazil. They were both electronic technicians. They bought matching suits. They bought raincoats. They purchased lead and tools to build crude masks shaped to cover their eyes.

They had a plan. And they followed it exactly.

A boy flying a kite on Vintem Hill found them lying side by side in the grass. Dressed identically. Lead masks positioned perfectly over their eyes. No signs of struggle. No visible injuries. They looked like they had laid down on purpose and waited.

The notebook was the detail that turned this from strange to unexplainable. Five handwritten instructions. The last two lines: "Protect metals. Wait for the signal."

What metals? What signal? From whom?

Toxicology was never properly completed. The coroner's office was backed up and the organs degraded before they could be tested. The capsules they were instructed to take were never identified. Whatever they swallowed, it killed them both.

Investigators eventually discovered that both men were involved in scientific spiritualism, a fringe practice in 1960s Brazil that blended occult rituals with electronics and pseudoscience. They believed they could make contact with extraterrestrial intelligence using specific equipment and substances.

They weren't the only ones. A third technician connected to their circle had been found dead under almost identical circumstances one year earlier. Same lead masks. Same ritualistic positioning.

Someone convinced these men to put on lead masks, swallow unknown capsules, and wait for something to happen. They believed it completely. They prepared for days. They followed every instruction to the letter.

The signal never came. Or maybe it did. We just don't know what it looked like.
