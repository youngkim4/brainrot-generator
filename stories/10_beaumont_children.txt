Three children took a five-minute bus ride to the beach on a summer morning in 1966. Multiple witnesses saw them playing with a tall blond stranger. They were laughing. They looked comfortable. They never came home.

Jane Beaumont was nine. Her sister Arnna was seven. Their brother Grant was four. On January 26th, Australia Day, their mother Nancy put them on the bus to Glenelg Beach near Adelaide. It was a trip they'd made before. She expected them back by noon.

At the beach, several witnesses noticed the three children with a man. Tall. Thin. Blond. Mid-thirties. He bought them pastries and a meat pie. They weren't scared of him. They were playing, laughing, completely at ease. One witness said Jane called him by name.

She knew him. She had met him before. And her parents had no idea he existed.

At twelve fifteen, Nancy walked to the bus stop. The children weren't on the noon bus. Or the next one. By two, she called her husband. By three, police were searching. By nightfall, the entire country was looking for the Beaumont children.

It was the largest investigation in Australian history at that time. Police searched storm drains, construction sites, vacant lots, the ocean floor. They received thousands of tips. They followed every lead.

They found nothing. Not a shoe. Not a hair ribbon. Not a thread from their clothing. Three children ceased to exist between the beach and the bus stop.

The blond man was never identified. Decades later, a suspect emerged. A wealthy local man with a history of predatory behavior and a property near the beach that contained an underground room. He died before charges could be brought. Excavations of his property were inconclusive.

Jane would be sixty-nine now. Arnna, sixty-seven. Grant, sixty-four. Their father Jim died in 2019 without ever learning what happened.

Their mother Nancy is still alive. She never moved from the family home. She never changed the phone number.

In case one of them calls.
