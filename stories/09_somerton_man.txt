An unidentified man was found dead on an Australian beach with a secret pocket sewn into his trousers. Inside was a scrap of paper that read "Taman Shud." It's Persian for "it is ended." A coded message linked to the case has never been cracked.

December 1st, 1948. Somerton Beach, Adelaide. Two men found a body propped against the seawall. Well-dressed. Clean-shaven. Mid-forties. No wallet. No identification. Every label had been cut from his clothing.

The autopsy found a man in perfect physical condition. His cause of death was consistent with poisoning, but no known poison was detected. His fingerprints matched no one in any database on earth. His dental records matched no one. He didn't exist in any system, anywhere.

Then they found the pocket. Not a regular pocket. A small concealed one, hand-stitched into the lining of his trousers. Inside it, tightly rolled, was a torn scrap of paper with two printed words: Taman Shud.

The scrap came from a specific copy of the Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam, a twelfth-century book of Persian poetry. Months later, a man turned up with the matching book, claiming he'd found it in his car. In the back cover, someone had penciled five lines of seemingly random letters.

Military codebreakers tried. Cryptographers tried. Amateurs have tried for seventy-five years. The code has never been solved.

A phone number written in the book belonged to a nurse named Jessica Thomson who lived half a mile from where the body was found. When police showed her a plaster cast of the dead man's face, she went pale and nearly fainted. She denied knowing him for the rest of her life.

In 2022, DNA analysis tentatively identified him as Carl Webb, an electrical engineer from Melbourne whose ex-wife had ties to intelligence work. But the identification remains disputed. The code remains unbroken. The poison remains unidentified.

And no one has explained why a man with no identity sewed a Persian farewell into a hidden pocket and went to die on a beach half a mile from a woman who claimed she'd never seen him before.
