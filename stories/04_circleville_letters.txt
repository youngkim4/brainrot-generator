For eighteen years, someone sent anonymous letters to the residents of a small Ohio town. The letters knew things no stranger should know. One man died trying to find the writer. Another was sent to prison. But from his cell, he kept receiving letters too.

It started in 1976. Circleville, Ohio. Population thirteen thousand. Letters began arriving with no return address, postmarked from Columbus. They named affairs. They described what people did behind closed doors and exactly when they did it. Someone was watching this town. And they wanted everyone to know.

The main target was Mary Gillispie, a school bus driver. The letters accused her of an affair with the school superintendent. They were sent to her husband. To her neighbors. They were stapled to telephone poles along her bus route where children could read them.

In 1977, Mary's husband Ron got a phone call. He grabbed his gun, told his kids he was going to confront the letter writer, and drove into the night. He was found dead hours later. His truck had hit a tree. His gun had been fired once. His blood alcohol was through the roof. His family swore he never drank.

The letters didn't stop. They got worse. They mocked Ron's death by name. They threatened Mary's children.

Then came the trap. A sign appeared along Mary's bus route. When she stopped to pull it down, she found a loaded handgun wired to the sign post, aimed to fire when the sign was removed. The gun was traced to Paul Freshour, Ron's brother-in-law. He was convicted and sent to prison.

But from his prison cell, Paul kept receiving letters. They taunted him. They told him he would never get out. They proved that whoever was writing them was still free. Still watching.

The letters stopped in the mid-nineties. No one was ever identified. Handwriting analysis was inconclusive. Recent investigations suggest there may have been three separate writers.

Whoever terrorized Circleville for almost two decades took their identity to the grave. And one question remains. How did they know so much about everyone?
