On Christmas Eve 1945, a house fire killed five children. But when investigators searched the ashes, they found no bones. No teeth. No remains of any kind. Twenty years later, the family received a photograph of a young man who looked exactly like their missing son.

The Sodder family of Fayetteville, West Virginia had nine children. George and Jennie and four of the kids escaped the fire. The other five, ages five to fourteen, were trapped on the second floor. The house burned flat in under an hour.

But a house fire hot enough to kill five children is hot enough to leave bones. Exposed cremation at a funeral home requires temperatures over fourteen hundred degrees sustained for hours, and it still leaves skeletal fragments. A forty-five-minute house fire doesn't reach those temperatures. Five children should have left remains.

There were none.

George Sodder hired a private investigator. What they found made the fire look less like an accident and more like a crime scene. The phone lines had been cut before the fire. Not burned. Severed. The ladder George kept against the house was missing. It was found two hundred feet away in a ditch. The fire chief's initial report blamed faulty wiring, but the house had been inspected and rewired months earlier.

A witness came forward saying she saw the children in a car being driven out of town the night of the fire. Another witness spotted them at a hotel in Charleston. Both were dismissed by local police.

Then in 1967, an envelope arrived with no return address. Inside was a photograph of a young man in his twenties. On the back, someone had written: "Louis Sodder. I love brother Frankie. Ilil Boys. A90132 or 35."

Louis was one of the missing children. The handwriting was never identified. The photo was never traced.

The Sodders put up a billboard with the children's faces that stayed up for decades. George died in 1969 without answers. Jennie kept the sign up until she died in 1989.

Five children vanished from a fire that left no trace of them. Someone sent a photo twenty years later. And no one has ever explained where those children went.
