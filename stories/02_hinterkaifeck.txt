A German farmer found footprints in the snow leading to his house. There were none leading away. Six days later, everyone inside was dead.

Andreas Gruber told his neighbors about the footprints. He mentioned hearing footsteps in the attic at night. A newspaper appeared on his property that nobody in the family had bought. His house keys vanished, then showed up again days later. He told no one else. He didn't call police.

On the night of March 31st, 1922, someone lured the family into the barn one at a time. Andreas first. Then his wife. Then their widowed daughter. Then her seven-year-old. Each one walked in and never walked out. They were killed with a mattock, a farming tool halfway between an axe and a pickaxe. The killer then went inside the house and murdered the maid and a two-year-old boy in their beds.

Six people. One weapon. Total silence. No one in the village heard a thing.

But here's the part that has kept investigators awake for a hundred years. The killer didn't leave. After murdering six people, they stayed on the farm. For days. They fed the livestock. They ate food from the kitchen. Neighbors saw smoke rising from the chimney and assumed the family was home.

Whoever did this slept in a house with six corpses. And they were in no hurry to leave.

When the bodies were finally discovered, the farm was in perfect order. Animals fed. Fire tended. Over a hundred suspects were investigated. The case was reopened four separate times with modern forensic technology. DNA. Psychological profiling. Crime scene reconstruction. None of it worked.

No one knows who hid in that attic. No one knows how long they were there before the murders. And no one knows where they went when they finally left Hinterkaifeck. Because just like those first footprints in the snow, the killer left no trail behind.

Someone walked to that farm. And they never walked away.
